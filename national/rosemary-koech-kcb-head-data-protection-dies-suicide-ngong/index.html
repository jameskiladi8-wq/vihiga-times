KCB Head of Data Protection Rosemary Koech Dies at 40; Autopsy Confirms Suicide

Rosemary Chemutai Koech Kimwatu, 40, Head of Data Protection at KCB Bank Group, died by suicide on August 21, 2026, at her Ngong home. An autopsy confirmed the cause of death as her family revealed she faced marital and financial difficulties.

25 August 2026 6 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 12:00 PM

KCB Head of Data Protection Rosemary Koech Dies at 40; Autopsy Confirms Suicide
Kenya's banking and technology sector is mourning the death of Rosemary Chemutai Koech Kimwatu, the Head of Data Protection at KCB Bank Group, who died on Friday, August 21, 2026, at her home in Ngong, Kajiado County.

An autopsy conducted by Chief Government Pathologist Dr Johansen Oduor at Lee Funeral Home established that the 40-year-old executive died by suicide. Police said she was found hanging from a bedroom window at her residence in Ole Nairi area, Kaiyet Close, Ngong.

What Has Been Confirmed
  • Name: Rosemary Chemutai Koech Kimwatu
  • Age: 40 years
  • Date of death: August 21, 2026
  • Location: Her home in Ngong, Kajiado County
  • Cause of death: Suicide (confirmed by autopsy)
  • Circumstance: Found hanging from a bedroom window; no suicide note recovered
  • Family statement: She faced marital and financial difficulties; father said KCB supported the family
  • Investigation: Police investigations are ongoing

Family Reveals Marital and Financial Difficulties

The family of Rosemary Koech has revealed that she had been experiencing marital and financial difficulties before her death. According to a statement from her relatives, she had complained of frustrations linked to debts she was servicing.

Her father, Paul Koech, broke his silence on the emerging narratives surrounding his daughter's death, saying the family had no doubts about the support she received from KCB Bank.

"We as the parents of Rosemary Koech have no doubts in our minds that KCB accorded Rosemary all the necessary space and support to thrive and be the best she could in her career ladder," Paul Koech said in a statement shared on social media.

He added that the bank stood with the family both during Rosemary's illness and following her death.

"In sickness and at her demise, KCB stood with the family and came through where help was required. We are forever grateful," he said.

Paul Koech appealed for restraint as the family mourns, stating that circulating narratives do not represent the family's position.

"Whatever narratives are going on does not represent the family stand and we wish to be allowed to mourn our dear child in peace," he said.

What Happened on the Day of Her Death

According to police, a caretaker at the residence said relatives broke down the bedroom door after Koech failed to respond to calls and knocks. Police processed the scene and said no suicide note was recovered. Her body was later moved to Lee Funeral Home for a postmortem examination.

Detectives and family members attended the autopsy examination. Police investigations into the circumstances surrounding the death are ongoing.

A Distinguished Career in Law, Technology and Data Protection

Rosemary Koech had built a distinguished career spanning nearly two decades in law, technology, public policy and data protection. She joined KCB in June 2022 as Data Protection Officer after serving as Public Policy Manager at Safaricom. In June 2023, she was promoted to Head of Data Protection, overseeing the Group's data protection compliance.

Before joining Safaricom, she held legal, regulatory and public policy roles in Kenya's technology sector, including positions at Oxigene.

Beyond her corporate roles, Rosemary was a prominent figure in Kenya's digital rights and technology policy community. She served as a board member of the Kenya ICT Action Network (KICTANet), Principal of the Kenya School of Internet Governance, and chaired multistakeholder advisory groups for the Kenya and East Africa Internet Governance Forums.

She was also a founding Vice President of the Data Privacy and Governance Society of Kenya and a trustee at KICTANet.

Tributes from the Tech and Banking Community

The Kenya ICT Action Network (KICTANet) and the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner were among the organisations that mourned her passing, describing her as warm, committed and central to building inclusive digital spaces in Kenya.

KICTANet announced her death, paying tribute to her role as a board member and champion of privacy and data governance. Her passing has left her family, colleagues and Kenya's digital rights community mourning the loss of a prominent data protection and technology policy expert.

Unverified Allegations Circulate Online

While official agencies have confirmed the autopsy findings, several online reports have made unverified claims linking her death to a combination of alleged personal and professional pressures. These reports, which have not been independently verified, mention marital separation, debt, heavy workload and psychological distress.

Some social media posts have alleged workplace mistreatment by her supervisor, claims that have not been confirmed by authorities or the bank. The family has not confirmed these claims and has asked for privacy and prayers for her four children.

Reputable reports have noted that the circumstances surrounding her death had not been fully publicly disclosed beyond the autopsy confirmation of suicide.

Mental Health Awareness for Professionals

The gap between a successful public life and hidden personal struggles has shocked many Kenyans and sparked conversations about mental health among professionals.

From outside, Rosemary had what many dream of - a senior position at a major bank, a good salary, professional respect and recognition in her field. But as psychologists say, success does not make anyone immune to depression, anxiety or burnout.

In Kenya, many people still suffer in silence, fearing being gossiped about, isolated at work, or being called weak if they speak about mental health challenges.

What Happens Next

Police investigations into the circumstances surrounding the death continue. The family has requested privacy as they mourn and care for her four children.

The data protection, technology policy and banking communities in Kenya are expected to hold memorial events and tributes to honour her contributions to the sector.
