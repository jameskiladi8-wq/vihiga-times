65-Year-Old Man Arrested in Vihiga Over Alleged Defilement of Grade 9 Pupil

A 65-year-old man from Lusuka Village, Mbihi Sub-location, has been arrested following public tip-offs over allegations involving a Grade 9 pupil. The suspect and pupil were taken to Mbale Police Station as investigations continue.

17 September 2026 5 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 2:00 PM

A 65-year-old man from Lusuka Village in Mbihi Sub-location, Vihiga County, has been arrested in connection with allegations involving a Grade 9 pupil from a nearby school.

The suspect, identified as Evans Ogada, was arrested on Thursday, September 17, 2026, following information provided by members of the public, with assistance from the area assistant chief.

Arrest and Police Processing

Following the arrest, both the suspect and the pupil were taken to Mbale Police Station, where statements were recorded as part of ongoing police investigations.

The pupil was subsequently taken to hospital for medical assessment and attention.

The suspect is expected to be presented before a court, where the allegations will be addressed through the legal process.

Circumstances Under Investigation

According to available information, the circumstances surrounding the alleged incident have not yet been fully established. The suspect was not reported to have been found committing the alleged offence.

At this stage, the suspect has not been convicted of any offence. The allegations remain subject to investigation and determination by the courts.

It is important to distinguish between an arrest, an allegation and a conviction. An arrest does not, on its own, establish that a person committed a crime.

Similarly, the circumstances of the alleged incident should not be assumed before investigators establish the facts and the matter is heard in court.

Public Reactions

The reported arrest has attracted various reactions from members of the public, with some calling for the law to take its course while others have questioned the circumstances surrounding the allegations.

However, speculation about the relationship between the suspect and the person who reported the matter cannot establish whether the allegations are true.

Questions about whether the pupil screamed or resisted also cannot, on their own, determine what happened. The circumstances of an alleged offence involving a child must be examined through proper investigations and the legal process.

Legal Framework on Defilement in Kenya

Under Kenya's Sexual Offences Act, defilement is a serious criminal offence.

The law provides that any person who commits an act that causes penetration with a child is guilty of the offence of defilement.

Penalties for defilement vary depending on the age of the child:

  • Defilement of a child aged 11 years or younger carries a minimum sentence of life imprisonment
  • Defilement of a child aged between 12 and 15 years carries a minimum sentence of not less than 20 years imprisonment
  • Defilement of a child aged between 16 and 18 years carries a minimum sentence of 15 years imprisonment

Recent court decisions in Kenya have shown that courts take defilement cases seriously. In September 2026, a 92-year-old man was sentenced to life imprisonment after being convicted of defiling a 10-year-old child in Mukurwe-ini, Nyeri County.

In another recent case, the Kabale High Court in Uganda sentenced a 26-year-old man to 14 years' imprisonment for aggravated defilement and aggravated trafficking in children.

What Happens Next

The suspect is expected to be presented before a court, where formal charges will be read and a plea entered.

Investigators will continue gathering evidence, including medical reports, witness statements and any other relevant information to support or refute the allegations.

The court will then determine whether there is sufficient evidence to proceed with trial and whether the suspect should be remanded in custody or released on bail pending trial.

Reporting Child Protection Concerns

Members of the public who suspect child abuse or defilement are encouraged to report to:

  • The nearest police station
  • The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI)
  • The Children's Department
  • Local administration (chiefs and assistant chiefs)
  • Child protection organisations

Kenya's child protection laws require that all cases involving children be handled with sensitivity and confidentiality to protect the child's welfare and dignity.
