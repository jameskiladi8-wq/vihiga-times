Record 3.6 Million Candidates to Sit 2026 National Exams Under Tighter Security

Kenya has registered 3,634,846 candidates for the 2026 KPSEA, KJSEA and KCSE examinations under tighter security measures.

6 October 2026 5 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 2:00 PM

Nairobi, Kenya — A record 3,634,846 candidates have been registered for Kenya's 2026 national assessments and examinations, which will be administered under stricter rules to protect their integrity.

The Kenya National Examinations Council will conduct two competency-based assessments — the Kenya Primary School Education Assessment and the Kenya Junior School Education Assessment — alongside the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education.

Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba said authorities would not tolerate examination malpractice.

"Let me affirm today that we will have no mercy on anyone who will be found engaging in any form of malpractice. All candidates, centre managers, supervisors and invigilators must comply with examination regulations, failing which they will be subjected to the full force of the law," Ogamba said.

Candidate numbers by assessment

The 2026 season is the largest KNEC has handled to date.

  • KPSEA: 1,380,463 learners
  • KJSEA: 1,206,028 learners
  • KCSE: 1,048,355 candidates

This is the first time KCSE candidature has exceeded one million. The growth in numbers means more resources and greater dedication from all involved in the field administration of the examinations, Ogamba said.

Vihiga County candidates will be included in these national totals. The county's schools should therefore prepare early to avoid congestion and administrative delays at examination centres.

Key Facts: 2026 National Examinations
  • Total candidates: 3,634,846 (a KNEC record)
  • KPSEA: 1,380,463 learners
  • KJSEA: 1,206,028 learners
  • KCSE: 1,048,355 candidates — first time above one million
  • Examiners recruited: 59,904 (vetted by TSC)
  • Security: Smart padlocks, armed officers, intelligence-led deployment
  • Source: Ministry of Education & KNEC season launch, October 2026

Examination timetable

KNEC has released the following key dates for the 2026 season:

  • KCSE rehearsals: Friday, October 16, 2026
  • KCSE papers with low candidature: October 19 to 30, 2026
  • KCSE papers with large candidature: November 2 to 20, 2026
  • KPSEA and KJSEA assessments: October 26 to 30, 2026

Centre managers, supervisors and invigilators are expected to follow these schedules strictly.

Smart padlocks and tighter controls

KNEC chief executive Dr David Njengere said all examination storage containers would be secured with smart padlocks, which were first piloted last year. The locks will be monitored from KNEC headquarters and will record when they are opened or closed and whether they have been interfered with.

Containers will be guarded around the clock by four armed security officers — two during the day and two at night.

"All candidates will see the papers for the first time on the day of the examination. I advise parents and candidates not to waste their money on promises that anyone can get them the papers before exam time," Njengere said.

To prevent morning and afternoon session papers from being mixed up, KNEC has packed the morning papers in white-labelled cartons with blue straps, while afternoon papers are in yellow-labelled cartons with black straps.

Question papers will also be personalised with candidates' details. The details will be removed at the end of each session so that examiners mark anonymised scripts.

Security deployment

Armed security officers will not be deployed to KPSEA and KJSEA centres as part of reforms aimed at reducing the stakes at this level.

However, two security officers will be assigned to each KCSE examination centre, with centres hosting more than 250 candidates receiving additional officers.

The National Intelligence Service and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations will also be involved during the examination season, as part of an intelligence-led approach to deter malpractices before they happen.

What Vihiga candidates and parents should do

Candidates in Vihiga should:

  • Arrive early on examination days
  • Carry only permitted materials
  • Follow instructions from centre managers, supervisors and invigilators

Parents should ensure learners have adequate rest, meals and transport arrangements. Schools should also ensure examination rooms, storage facilities and security arrangements meet KNEC requirements.

The record candidature means examination centres across the country, including those in Vihiga County, should prepare early to avoid congestion and administrative delays.

Examiners and contingency plans

The Teachers Service Commission has identified and vetted 59,904 examiners to mark the assessments and examinations. Acting TSC chief executive Evaleen Mitei warned that teachers involved in examination malpractice would face disciplinary action.

The Ministry of Interior said all counties had drawn contingency plans for disruptions that could be caused by above-average rainfall associated with the El Niño phenomenon. Field officers from relevant ministries and agencies would be on standby to provide support where required.

Officials involved in the examinations and assessments began mandatory briefings at sub-county level, conducted by KNEC officers. Delegation was not allowed.

What Parents, Schools and Candidates Should Prepare For
  • Ensure learners arrive early on examination days and carry only permitted materials.
  • Plan for adequate rest, meals and transport before and during the examination period.
  • Schools should confirm that examination rooms, storage facilities and security arrangements meet KNEC requirements.
  • Follow all instructions from centre managers, supervisors and invigilators.
  • Report any suspected malpractice to KNEC or the relevant authorities.
  • Watch official Ministry of Education and KNEC channels for timetable updates and contingency announcements.
