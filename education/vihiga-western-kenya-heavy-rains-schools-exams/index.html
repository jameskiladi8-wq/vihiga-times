Heavy Rains May Disrupt Learning and National Exams in Vihiga and Western Kenya

Vihiga is among 43 counties forecast to receive wetter-than-normal rainfall, with risks to classrooms, transport and national examinations.

27 August 2026 5 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 12:00 PM

Vihiga County, Kenya — Schools in Vihiga and other flood-prone parts of Kenya have been warned to prepare for possible disruption as heavy rains are expected during the October to December season.

The Kenya Meteorological Department has forecast that 43 counties could receive wetter-than-normal rainfall. Vihiga, Kakamega, Bungoma and Busia are among the counties listed as potential hotspots.

The warning is particularly important because the Kenya Primary School Education Assessment, the Kenya Junior School Education Assessment and the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education examinations are scheduled during the rainy season. Flooded roads, damaged classrooms and disrupted transport could affect learners' attendance and the movement of examination materials.

Risks to schools and learners

Heavy rainfall can affect schools in several ways:

  • Flooding of classrooms, toilets and playgrounds
  • Damage to roofs, walls, desks and learning materials
  • Poor road access for learners, teachers and examination officials
  • Increased health risks, including waterborne diseases
  • Disruption of revision programmes and examination preparation

The Ministry of Education says it has put measures in place to ensure examination materials reach centres despite the difficult conditions, including the use of helicopters and drones. But it has also acknowledged that some examination centres could be forced to close if flooding makes them unsafe or inaccessible.

"We may be forced to close some exam centres. In Wajir, where flooding often cuts off roads by the afternoon, schools can consider having pupils report as early as 6 am and end the school day early before the heaviest rain sets in," said Dr Kanyi Gioko, Director of Administration at the Ministry of Education.

Such measures could become increasingly important as communities in high-risk areas contend with flooded roads, damaged bridges and disrupted transport.

Key Facts: Heavy Rains and National Exams
  • Forecast period: October to December rainy season
  • Counties affected: 43 counties expected to receive wetter-than-normal rainfall
  • Western Kenya hotspots: Vihiga, Kakamega, Bungoma, Busia
  • Exams at risk: KPSEA, KJSEA and KCSE (all scheduled during the rainy season)
  • Ministry of Education response: Helicopters and drones to deliver examination materials
  • Possible action: Some exam centres may be closed if flooding makes them unsafe or inaccessible

What schools should do

School administrators should inspect roofs, drainage systems, walkways and classroom structures before the rains intensify. They should also identify safe evacuation routes and ensure that learners know what to do during heavy rain or flooding.

Parents should monitor weather updates, ensure children have safe transport arrangements and avoid sending learners through flooded roads or rivers.

Teachers should prepare alternative revision materials in case lessons are interrupted. Schools hosting examination centres should also work closely with education officials to protect examination materials and ensure candidates can access centres safely.

The Kenya Red Cross has warned that flooding could also trigger landslides, mudslides and rockfalls in hilly areas, while creating conditions favourable for outbreaks of water-borne and vector-borne diseases.

Why it matters for Vihiga

Vihiga's hilly terrain and seasonal rainfall can make some routes difficult during heavy downpours. Early planning can reduce the risk of learners missing school or examinations.

The most important step is early preparedness: schools, parents and local leaders should act before flooding occurs rather than waiting for disruption to happen.

The agency has warned of potential large-scale displacement in some of the country's highest-risk areas, including parts of the Lake Victoria basin. For affected families, displacement could have consequences beyond the loss of homes and livelihoods.

Some learners may be forced to travel long distances or sit their examinations in unfamiliar centres if schools and examination centres have to be relocated.

What Parents, Schools and Candidates Should Do
  • Inspect roofs, drainage systems, walkways and classroom structures before rains intensify.
  • Identify safe evacuation routes and brief learners on what to do during flooding.
  • Monitor official weather updates from the Kenya Meteorological Department.
  • Avoid sending children through flooded roads, rivers or bridges.
  • Prepare alternative revision materials in case lessons are disrupted.
  • Schools hosting exam centres should coordinate closely with education officials.
  • Report unsafe or flooded routes to local authorities and school management.
