Bursaries Return to Vihiga With Sh100 Million Allocation After One-Year Suspension

Vihiga County has restored its bursary programme with a Sh100 million allocation in the 2026/27 budget after a one-year suspension.

26 September 2026 6 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 12:00 PM

Vihiga County, Kenya — Vihiga County has reinstated its bursary programme with a Sh100 million allocation in the 2026/27 financial year, following a one-year suspension that left parents and students seeking answers.

The allocation was included in the Sh7.72 billion county budget approved by the Vihiga County Assembly on June 28, 2026. The fund is intended to support bright and needy students across the county's sub-counties.

The reinstatement reverses the decision to halt the programme during the 2025/26 financial year. However, the budget documents and reports reviewed for this story do not explain in detail why the bursary programme was suspended or how the county assessed the resulting gap in student support.

Return of a campaign promise

The bursary programme is linked to Governor Wilber Ottichilo's 2022–2027 education agenda. His manifesto and inaugural address promised to expand the county bursary and Governor's Scholarship Programme and continue supporting students, including those pursuing technical and vocational education.

The 2022 address also acknowledged that the existing bursary was inadequate because of high demand and called for increased funding for the governor's scholarship programme.

The county government says its education support framework also includes scholarships and supplementary grants for eligible students in secondary schools and vocational training centres. The county's Education Bill, 2025, was later assented to as an Act, providing a legal framework for scholarships and education grants.

Key Details of the Bursary Reinstatement
  • Allocation: Sh100 million for 2026/27 financial year
  • Approved in: Sh7.72 billion county budget (Vihiga County Assembly, 28 June 2026)
  • Previous Suspension: Programme halted during 2025/26 financial year
  • Ward-Level Arrangement (Previous): Sh4 million per ward across 25 wards
  • Education Department Budget: Sh594 million in the approved county budget
  • Legal Framework: Vihiga County Education Act (2025)

How much will students receive?

The approved allocation amounts to Sh100 million for the financial year. Previous county disbursement arrangements reportedly gave each ward Sh4 million, which would account for the full Sh100 million across Vihiga's 25 wards.

That figure should not automatically be treated as the amount each student will receive. The final benefit per learner will depend on the number of approved applicants, the county's eligibility criteria and the distribution formula used by the relevant education offices.

The county had not, in the information reviewed, published the number of students expected to benefit from the 2026/27 allocation or the date when applications and disbursements would begin.

Wider education budget

The Department of Education and Technical Vocational Training received Sh594 million in the approved county budget. The overall budget is financed through equitable share, grants and projected own-source revenue, with Sh5.26 billion allocated to recurrent expenditure and Sh2.32 billion for development programmes.

The county has also said it will focus on completing stalled projects instead of launching new ones. In the health sector, the budget includes Sh23 million for projects such as Sabatia Maternity Ward, Kegondi Health Centre and Iduku Health Centre.

The education budget is therefore being implemented alongside wider financial pressures, including pending bills and the need to fund existing county programmes.

Separate from NGCDF bursaries

The county bursary is different from bursaries supported through the National Government Constituencies Development Fund.

NG-CDF bursary programmes are administered at constituency level, while the Vihiga County bursary is a county-government education intervention. A student may therefore encounter different application procedures, eligibility requirements and payment timelines depending on the fund being applied for.

The two systems should not be combined when assessing how much support is available to students in Vihiga. The county's Sh100 million allocation refers to the county programme and does not represent the total value of all bursary support available through other public or private schemes.

Scholarship and TVET support

The county's mid-term review reported that more than 420 students had benefited from the Governor's Scholarship Programme, with 98 per cent reportedly transitioning to university. It also reported an increase in ECDE classrooms from 229 in 2022 to 250 in 2025.

Ottichilo's education agenda included strengthening five specialised model TVET institutions, establishing more vocational training centres, creating innovation and business hubs and introducing a youth business start-up fund. The bursary programme was also presented as a way of supporting students in technical and vocational training.

The available information confirms the reinstatement of the bursary allocation, but does not establish the current implementation status of every TVET and youth-finance promise.

Questions for the County
  • Why was the bursary programme suspended in 2025/26?
  • How many students will benefit from the Sh100 million allocation?
  • What criteria will be used to identify bright and needy applicants?
  • Will each ward receive an equal amount?
  • When will applications open and disbursements begin?
  • How will the county prevent delays or duplication with other bursary programmes?

For parents and learners

These details will determine whether the reinstated allocation reaches students on time and whether it is enough to meet demand.

For parents and learners, the return of the bursary is welcome. But after a year without the county fund, they are likely to judge the programme by the transparency of the application process, the number of beneficiaries and the speed of payment.
