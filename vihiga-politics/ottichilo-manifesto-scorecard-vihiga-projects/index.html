From Promise to Delivery: How Far Has Ottichilo's 2022 Manifesto Gone?

Vihiga's CIDP mid-term review reports progress in health, water, roads, agriculture and education, but audit findings point to unfinished projects and delivery gaps.

25 September 2026 9 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 12:00 PM

Vihiga County, Kenya — Governor Wilber Ottichilo's second and final term is entering its last year, leaving Vihiga residents with a central question: how much of the 2022 campaign agenda has been delivered?

Ottichilo's manifesto promised improvements in healthcare, water supply, roads, education, agriculture and youth empowerment. The County Integrated Development Plan (CIDP) 2023–2027, which guides the administration's development agenda, also set targets for infrastructure expansion, service delivery and economic transformation.

A mid-term review launched in June 2026 provides the county government's assessment of progress. It reports that more than 600 projects worth Sh2.8 billion had been implemented during the review period, with about 84 per cent completed and the remainder at different stages.

The report also placed performance of planned objectives at about 90 per cent. However, another account of the review launch reported overall implementation of development targets at approximately 80 per cent. The difference appears to reflect different measures used in assessing objectives and projects.

Health promises show mixed progress

Health was one of the most prominent areas in Ottichilo's manifesto. The pledges included completing the Hospital Plaza at Vihiga County Referral Hospital, upgrading sub-county hospitals to Level 4, improving the referral hospital towards Level 5 status, completing Givigoi Sub-County Hospital and strengthening supplies, staffing and equipment in health facilities.

The mid-term review reports that the number of public health facilities increased from 74 in 2022 to 78 in 2025. It also says the average distance to the nearest health facility fell from three kilometres to 2.3 kilometres.

The county further reported completing a 97-capacity funeral home, a 44-bed Mother and Child Unit and the Hospital Plaza at Vihiga County Referral Hospital.

The Hospital Plaza claim, however, requires careful qualification. An audit report reviewed by Parliament found that the Sh395 million project was 56 per cent complete despite the contract having expired in May 2024, with no evidence of renewal. That finding conflicts with the mid-term review's description of the project as completed.

The 2026/27 county budget allocated Sh23 million for stalled health projects, including works at Sabatia Maternity Ward, Kegondi Health Centre and Iduku Health Centre. This suggests that some health infrastructure commitments still require additional funding or completion.

Health Sector: Key Figures
  • Public health facilities: Up from 74 (2022) to 78 (2025)
  • Average distance to facility: Down from 3 km to 2.3 km
  • Reported completions: 97-capacity funeral home, 44-bed Mother and Child Unit, Hospital Plaza
  • Hospital Plaza audit finding: Sh395M project 56% complete despite contract expiry (May 2024)
  • 2026/27 budget allocation: Sh23M for stalled health projects (Sabatia, Kegondi, Iduku)

Water access improves, but coverage is incomplete

Ottichilo's manifesto promised reliable clean water for households, rehabilitation of distribution networks and protection of water sources.

The mid-term review reports that access to clean water rose from 64 per cent to 68.8 per cent. It also says the county established 28 community water projects and solarised 32 water schemes.

The average time spent fetching water reportedly fell from 30 minutes to 20 minutes. These figures point to progress, but also show that more than 30 per cent of residents remained outside the reported clean-water coverage level during the review period.

A separate report on the review launch said the county had implemented more than 28 community water projects. Governor Ottichilo attributed the wider progress to county efforts and support from development partners.

Roads: more access, limited bitumen upgrades

The governor's manifesto promised to improve rural roads to all-weather standards, identify roads for upgrading to bitumen standard and introduce performance-based maintenance for county roads.

The mid-term review records rehabilitation of 79 kilometres of roads and the opening of 228.6 kilometres of new access roads. The county says the work has improved links to markets, schools and health facilities.

The available county planning documents, however, set a more specific target of upgrading at least five kilometres per sub-county to bitumen standard. The figures supplied for implementation show 3.2 kilometres of roads paved under the labour-based road maintenance programme and 3.48 kilometres upgraded to bitumen standard.

On that measure, the reported performance falls below the five-kilometre target. The figures also do not by themselves establish whether all roads covered by the performance-based maintenance promise were maintained under a formal programme.

Education support was interrupted

The manifesto promised expanded bursaries and scholarships, stronger technical and vocational training institutions, youth business support and a pilot ECDE feeding programme.

The mid-term review reports that ECDE classrooms increased from 229 in 2022 to 250 in 2025. More than 420 students benefited from the Governor's Scholarship Programme, with 98 per cent reportedly transitioning to university.

The bursary pledge, however, faced a setback. The programme was halted during the 2025/26 financial year before being reintroduced in the 2026/27 budget with a Sh100 million allocation, according to the supplied research.

The interruption means the commitment to expand education support was not implemented continuously during the period under review. The reintroduction provides funding for the final phase of the county's current plan, but its impact will depend on how the allocation is administered and how many learners benefit.

Agriculture records measurable gains

The county's development agenda also focused on moving farmers from subsistence production towards commercial agriculture.

The mid-term review reports that maize productivity rose from eight bags per acre to 15 bags after the distribution of subsidised fertiliser, certified seed and other farm inputs. It also records the distribution of 548 in-calf dairy heifers and 16,580 chicks to poultry farmers.

The county further reported establishing 450 fish ponds to support food security and household incomes.

These figures represent reported outputs rather than a full assessment of household incomes, market access or the long-term survival of the livestock and fish-production projects. Those questions will be important in determining whether the programmes have delivered lasting economic benefits.

Manifesto Scorecard: Progress Snapshot
  • Health: More facilities reported, but Hospital Plaza status contested by audit findings
  • Water: Access up from 64% to 68.8%; 28 community projects, 32 solarised schemes
  • Roads: 79 km rehabilitated, 228.6 km new access roads; bitumen upgrades below target
  • Education: ECDE classrooms up from 229 to 250; bursary programme interrupted then reintroduced
  • Agriculture: Maize yields up from 8 to 15 bags/acre; 548 dairy heifers, 16,580 chicks, 450 fish ponds
  • Funding: Own-source revenue ~Sh398M (2024/25); pending bills disputed figures

Funding and unfinished projects remain a challenge

The mid-term review identified delayed releases from the National Treasury and funding gaps as obstacles to implementation. Governor Ottichilo also said conditional grants had declined sharply and development expenditure had been affected by delays in exchequer releases.

The county reported improved own-source revenue, which rose to nearly Sh398 million in the 2024/25 financial year. Another report citing the mid-term review said pending bills had declined from Sh1.46 billion to Sh839.3 million. That figure differs from the larger pending-bills figure discussed in earlier Senate scrutiny, underlining the need to identify the reporting period and accounting category attached to each number.

Audit findings also remain relevant to the delivery scorecard. Besides the Hospital Plaza, an audit review found that 89 per cent of the Sh58.7 million revenue automation contract had been paid while important deliverables, including system integration, training and security installation, were incomplete.

The final test

The mid-term review presents progress in several areas: more reported health facilities, increased water access, new and rehabilitated roads, expanded ECDE infrastructure, scholarship beneficiaries and agricultural support.

But the scorecard is not complete. Questions remain over the actual status of the Hospital Plaza, the extent of bitumen road upgrades, the interruption of bursary support, incomplete systems and the county's ability to finance unfinished projects.

The County Integrated Development Plan lists completion of ongoing health infrastructure, upgrading of sub-county hospitals, improved roads, clean water, education support and agricultural development among its priorities.

With the governor's second term ending in 2027, the remaining period will be measured less by the number of projects announced than by the completion and operation of those already started.

For residents, the key test is whether reported achievements are visible and functional at ward level—and whether unfinished promises receive clear timelines, funding and public reporting before the end of the term.
