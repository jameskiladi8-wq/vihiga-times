VTRH Receives Essential Medicines After Shortage Concerns in Vihiga

Vihiga Teaching and Referral Hospital has received essential medicines and non-pharmaceutical supplies from MEDS after patients raised concerns about shortages. The consignment is expected to improve access as the facility serves rising numbers from Vihiga and neighbouring counties.

9 September 2026 4 min read By Vihiga Times Reporter Updated 4:00 PM

Vihiga Teaching and Referral Hospital has received a new consignment of essential medicines and non-pharmaceutical supplies, offering relief to patients after recent concerns over the availability of basic drugs.

The supplies arrived at the facility on Wednesday, September 9, 2026, from Mission for Essential Drugs and Supplies (MEDS). The delivery is expected to support the hospital as it continues serving patients from Vihiga County and neighbouring areas.

Supplies Arrive After Patient Concerns

The consignment comes after some patients reported leaving the hospital without receiving all the medicines they needed. The concerns were linked partly to an increase in the number of patients seeking treatment at VTRH.

The hospital has been receiving patients from across the region, placing additional pressure on its medicines and other medical resources. The administration had indicated that efforts were underway to secure additional supplies.

The arrival of the latest consignment marks a step towards addressing some of those concerns, although the available information does not specify the types or quantities of medicines delivered.

Hospital Officials Oversee Delivery

The supplies were received under the supervision of VTRH Chief Executive Officer Titus Mumia.

He was accompanied by Deputy Director of Clinical Services Dr Collins Masika, Deputy Director of Corporate Services Clement Aderi, Deputy Director of Nursing Services William Litu and Pharmacy In-Charge Dr Duncan Matini.

The delivery has also been linked to efforts by officials in the county health sector and county administration, including County Executive Committee Member for Health Kitungulu Nicholas, Madam Marry and Fred Muhindi Mavisi.

Key Details of the VTRH Medicine Delivery
  • Facility: Vihiga Teaching and Referral Hospital (VTRH)
  • Supplies From: Mission for Essential Drugs and Supplies (MEDS)
  • Date Received: Wednesday, September 9, 2026
  • CEO Overseeing: Titus Mumia
  • Purpose: Address medicine shortages and rising patient numbers
  • Supplies: Essential medicines and non-pharmaceutical commodities

VTRH Serves Patients from Wider Region

The development is significant because VTRH has continued to serve a large number of patients, including people from outside Vihiga County.

The facility was also reported to have continued providing services during the recent nurses' strike, giving patients seeking urgent medical attention an alternative when services at some facilities were disrupted. However, the increased demand has also placed pressure on hospital staff, medicines and other supplies.

For patients and families, the new delivery is expected to reduce the need to look elsewhere for basic medicines. Whether it will provide a lasting solution will depend on the consistency of future supplies and the hospital's ability to manage rising patient numbers.

MEDS Role in Health Supplies

MEDS is a faith-based health solutions provider established through a partnership between the Kenya Conference of Catholic Bishops and the Christian Health Association of Kenya.

The organisation supports access to health products and related services for health facilities in Kenya. In the case of VTRH, its latest delivery is intended to strengthen the hospital's stock of essential medicines and non-pharmaceutical commodities.

Patients Hope for Consistent Availability

The arrival of the supplies has offered encouragement to residents who depend on VTRH for treatment. It also provides some relief to health workers who have continued serving patients despite pressure on the facility's resources.

However, the delivery does not mean all challenges facing the hospital have been resolved. Patients will be watching to see whether essential medicines remain available in the coming weeks and whether future shortages can be avoided.

For now, the consignment is a positive development for Vihiga residents and patients from the wider region who rely on the county's main referral facility.
